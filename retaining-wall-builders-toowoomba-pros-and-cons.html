Approval checks that change the job

Approval risk should be settled early rather than discovered after holes are dug. The source brief flags retained fill above 1 metre, nearness within 1.5 metres of a building or another wall, and surcharge loads as prompts to ask what must be confirmed for the actual address. Infrastructure and a fence above the wall can also introduce additional checks.

  • Height is not the whole story: a low face below fill can still matter if loads, drainage or access complicate the work.
  • Written scope beats verbal comfort: ask whether engineering, approvals and GST are included, excluded or pending.
  • Keep material taste separate: timber, concrete sleeper and block can each trigger the same site questions when conditions are constrained.

None of this means every wall follows one pathway. It means the owner should ask the provider or a certifier what applies before construction begins.

Material choice is the second decision

Once the site facts are clear, compare systems against exposure, maintenance expectation, access and the drainage method behind the face. Concrete sleeper discussions should cover sleeper profile, post spacing, finish and discharge path. Block walls need levels, curves, corners, foundations and compliant drainage checked plainly. Timber sleeper work deserves a candid talk about exposure, lifespan target and upkeep, especially where a smaller first scope is tempting.

Quote line items that hide value

Compare the same scope beside each provider, not merely the same length. The source brief lists excavation, foundations or posts, wall materials, drainage, backfill, corners, spoil, engineering, approvals, GST and reinstatement as the items that decide whether numbers are comparable. If several are unstated, a cheaper figure may be a narrower job.

  • State whether access assumes hand work or machine access.
  • State whether spoil removal is included.
  • State whether reinstatement means rough finish only or affected surfaces too.
  • State whether photos before inspection are enough for a firm price.

Quotes, prices, rates and estimates are indicative only and subject to individual circumstances and revision because the property governs access, approvals and drainage detail.

When repair may beat replacement

A leaning, cracked or locally damaged wall is not automatically a demolition job. The useful question is what evidence would support localised repair: the failed zone, the drainage behaviour, the load above and whether the rest of the face is stable. For replacement, the existing wall changes the job before material is chosen, so ask how removal, temporary support, spoil handling and the new drainage backed system will be sequenced.

  3. Confirm triggers. Check retained fill over a metre, closeness within 1.5 metres of structures and surcharge loads for the address.
  4. Send one brief. Give every builder the same measurements, photos and drainage notes so proposals can be compared fairly.
  5. Compare scope. Match excavation, drainage, spoil, approvals, GST and reinstatement line by line before deciding.

Common questions

Is the lowest retaining wall quote usually the best value? Not when scopes differ. A lower number can exclude excavation detail, drainage, spoil, engineering, approvals, GST or reinstatement. Match inclusions first, then compare totals.

Do timber sleeper walls avoid approval questions? No. Material preference is separate from site complexity. Timber can raise the same retained height, nearby structure, surcharge, drainage and access questions as concrete sleeper or block work.

Can a damaged wall be repaired instead of replaced? Sometimes. Ask what evidence supports localised repair, including the failed area, drainage behaviour, loads above and stability of the remaining face. Replacement needs a clear removal and support sequence.
